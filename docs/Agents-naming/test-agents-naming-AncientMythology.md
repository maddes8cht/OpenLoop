# Test Agents — Naming

These are the naming proposals for the test generation agents (equivalent to the documentation agents).

While the docs writers draw their names from the canon of *Deutsche Dichter und Denker*, the naming theme for the test agents is:

***Ancient Mythology & Sacred Languages*** — a cross-cultural blend of Greek myth, Sanskrit/Arabic, and Latin/Slavic roots. Each name carries a semantic resonance with its role: transformation, purity, truth, justice, wisdom.

## The winners

| Role                      | Name          | Rationale                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prep / Analysis**        | **Proteus**   | The Greek sea god who changes shape at will. He sees all forms a problem can take and reveals what lies beneath the surface — ideal for gap analysis.          |
| **Writer / Author**        | **Amala**     | Sanskrit for "pure", also an Arabic name meaning "hope". She writes clean, honest tests — uncorrupted by false assumptions, aspiring toward completeness.       |
| **Auditor / Reviewer**     | **Vera**      | Latin for "truth", Slavic for "faith". She verifies without mercy. Her name is her mandate: only what is true passes.                                         |
| **Final / Final Gate**     | **Dike**      | Greek goddess of justice and moral order. She weighs the work of the entire run and pronounces the final verdict — keep, keep partial, or discard.              |

## Alternatives

### Hermes as Finalizer (was in the running)

- **Proteus** (Prep)
- **Amala** (Writer)
- **Vera** (Auditor)
- **Hermes** (Final) — messenger of the gods, psychopomp who escorts souls to their final destination. A strong candidate for the finalization role (delivering the report, guiding the outcome to its resting place). Ultimately **Dike** was preferred because the final gate is a *judgment*, not a *delivery*.

### Janus as Prep (was in the running)

- **Janus** (Prep) — the two-faced Roman god of beginnings, transitions, and doorways. Looks both backward (existing tests) and forward (what is missing). A compelling choice for the preparation phase. Ultimately **Proteus** was preferred because the prep agent must *adapt* its analysis to whatever repository it encounters, not merely observe two directions.

### Sophia as Auditor or Finalizer (was in the running)

- **Proteus** (Prep)
- **Amala** (Writer)
- **Sophia** (Auditor) — Greek for "wisdom". A strong candidate for the auditor role, representing the wise, discerning eye that evaluates the work. Ultimately **Vera** ("truth") was preferred for the auditor because the audit is fundamentally about *verifying factual truth* (coverage, passing tests, edge cases) rather than exercising philosophical wisdom.
- **Dike** (Final)
  *or*
- **Sophia** (Final) — As the final gate, "wisdom" is a compelling concept for the ultimate verdict. Ultimately **Dike** ("justice") was preferred because the final disposition is a *judgment* on the work's merit and value to the repository, not just a wise observation.

### Combined alternatives

- Janus (Prep)
- Amala (Writer)
- Sophia (Auditor)
- Dike (Final)

*or*

- Proteus (Prep)
- Amala (Writer)
- Vera (Auditor)
- Sophia (Final)
